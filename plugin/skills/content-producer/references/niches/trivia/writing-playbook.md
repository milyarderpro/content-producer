## Apply the current config branch first (CR-062)

For config2, read [the generic profile workflow](formats.md) and the exact pinned
catalog guidance/examples before using this reference. Its global counts,
five-format/two-density list, default hook, single-goal CTA, legacy budget/loop
formula and shock-heavy example selection are **config1 fallback only**.
Config2 review follows its resolved profile: medium/context facts can earn their

Shared source-meaning, semantic dedup, scope, repair and readback safeguards below

Use with [content DNA](content-dna.md), the [pattern index](reference-patterns.md),

CR-061 changes the writer's process, not the three server attestations.
This playbook also applies to unsaved copy previews: omit business Write and
saved-state claims, while completing the same research, selection and review.
Examples teach selection and rhythm; production needs independent source
assessment and current semantic dedup. Keep brief selection notes internal;
do not expose long reasoning or paste source passages into public copy.

## 1. Resolve intent and choose an angle

Resolve owner scope, Page, enabled niche, requested draft count and theme.
Read generation context for allowed topics/formats, fact count and budgets.
Five drafts means five plan items; it does not imply `fixed: 5` facts.
Honor explicit fixed count/title/layout and the current item config.
For config1, prefer 7–8 fast facts or 5–6 utility facts only when allowed.
Config2 follows its resolved density/count preferences instead.

For config1, before choosing the angle, read DNA and the pattern index. Before composing,
read two relevant cards and one relevant anti-example. If only one card uses
the exact format, choose a second by reader payoff or voice; keep the requested
subject and valid format. Do not copy the example's facts, sequence or count.
State a short aim: what familiar thing will the reader see differently?
Body, food, places, history and objects deserve the same curiosity as animals.
Use recent variety for a batch, without rigid category quotas.


Before assigning a plan item's format, identify the device the reader will
actually see: one subject, a useful action/condition, a corrected belief,
observed social behavior, or a meaningful comparison. If the device is not
known yet and the user did not fix it, keep format_key: null in the plan
and choose a valid format when composing. Do not invent a comparison to
satisfy a distribution weight or rescue a mistaken label with the CTA.
A varied collection about one country can use single_subject_fast.
An explicit format or non-null item intent must be resolved honestly before
save; never silently change it or modify a completed plan item.

## 2. Research extra candidates and dedup

Research more candidates than the final count; open real supporting pages.
Record each atomic claim, its source URL, material qualifiers and a short
contribution note. One page may support several claims; its order is not the
card's order. A competitor POST-id is pattern provenance, never fact evidence.
Replace unsupported candidates rather than weakening their claims.

Run `trivia_find_similar_facts_v2` before final copy. Review all matches and
completeness; use eligible SAME canonical IDs. Replace used, uncertain,
ineligible or clipped/incomplete candidates and search replacements.
Different reader reactions do not prove semantic DISTINCT; never force it.
Do not reserve facts separately. If support/meaning remains valid after a
catalog conflict, refresh dedup review without needless source research.

## 3. Curate a set and order attention

Read all candidate lines together. Ask what each adds beyond the previous line.
Keep distinct, understandable reveals or useful conditions; remove obvious
filler, repeated measurements and repeated emotional beats. No fixed recipe
requires a number, history detail or emotional line in every card.
Research a replacement when the problem is selection; adjectives cannot fix it.

Test the set by its reader takeaways, not just distinct propositions. Briefly
summarize each line's payoff internally. If several reduce to the same idea
(such as "adults help the young"), keep the strongest and find different
supported angles on the requested theme. Different helpers or verbs do not
create a new reader experience. Do not impose a fixed quota of fact types.

Apply this test to places and objects too. Several routes, buildings or
facilities may all reduce to one experience, even with different locations
or measurements. Several origin/name details may all explain the same label.
One area, height or date without an interesting consequence is a weak ending.
A supplied research pool is not a checklist: rank the candidates and leave
weak ones out. With adaptive config, a shorter legal, varied set is preferable
to padding toward the preferred count; never reduce an explicit fixed count.
If two adjacent facts are retained as a contrast, make the meaningful
like-for-like relationship visible rather than merely changing their names.

In a broad cooperation card, separate babysitting, feeding young and teaching
young lines repeat the same care beat. Keep the strongest care reveal and
research other cooperative behaviors instead. Do not relabel the three as
"care", "food" and "learning" to pass this audit. This is a redundancy
diagnosis, not a ban on an explicit request comparing childcare roles.

Repeat the audit across all functions, not just childcare. Several lines
about call types, contact signals and a guard's warning may reduce to
"they communicate to stay safe/together." Keep the strongest reveal,
then select a different supported ability, habit or social behavior within
the user's scope. Do not swap one repetitive cluster for another.

Reject ordinary part/function or classification lines that only explain
what the subject is. Ask what a reader would retell to a friend. A substance
having "a natural compound" or a part doing its usual job is not a reveal.
Several such lines require new candidates, not friendlier synonyms.
For history, dates and inventors may be material, but patent → inventor →
date → successor is not an attention order. Start with an unexpected use,
failure, change or consequence; vary the middle and finish with a different
payoff. Preserve actual chronology within a claim.
Remove dates and names temporarily when reviewing the order. If the middle
still says early failure → redesign → adoption, it remains a timeline.
Move a stronger reveal forward and replace connecting steps with different
supported uses, origins, personal quirks or consequences. Do not merely
remove dates from the same sequence.

Open with the strongest independent reveal. Vary the middle and close with a
different memorable payoff. Move a useful context line later or replace it
when it makes a weak opening. Preserve necessary procedural order.
Comparison is like-for-like within each pair, with different dimensions allowed
between lines. A literal data table explicitly requested by the user stays a
data table; do not pretend its repeated metric is an accidental writing defect.
A comparison heading cannot turn separate descriptions into comparisons.
Choose features with a reason to be compared beyond being different.
A contrast can occupy one line, adjacent lines or an unmistakable common
baseline; do not force two cities into each sentence. Like-for-like scope
still applies to every metric comparison. A sequence of ordinary facilities
is not rescued by alternating city names or repeating a semicolon template.

## 4. Write the exact public copy

Name the subject in the opening after a generic hook; do not begin with an
unclear "adults" or "they". Keep brief scientific terms when understandable
and useful. Naturalness is not a ban on every technical noun. A lighter
context line can give breathing room when the overall set stays distinctive;
do not replace an accepted line just to maximize surprise on every line.
Conversely, an unrelated career detail or an ordinary adoption milestone
does not become valuable merely because it belongs to a different category.

Use reveal-first American English: concrete subjects, active familiar verbs,
natural contractions and one principal proposition per line. Remove jargon
and attribution only when they are immaterial. Preserve negation, numbers,
species, conditions, time, population and uncertainty. Never turn "some trained
dogs" into "dogs" to make a line shorter. Replace a fact if honest wording
cannot fit the current budget.
When only light wording changes are requested on an accepted structure,
keep the fact set and order. Use familiar verbs and descriptions instead
of unnecessary classification terms or textbook process wording; preserve
the actual mechanism. Do not regenerate the whole card to avoid line editing.

Check newly added causal words such as "because", "using" and "as" against
the source: a plausible mechanism or animal intention is not evidence.
Keep "usually", "some", visible-versus-hidden distinctions, geographic
boundaries and weather or other material conditions when the source needs
them. An estimate must not become an exact equality. A contrast such as
"despite its name" must retain the actual name that creates the contrast.
If the source supports only an observation, do not add a causal explanation.


Before attesting meaning_preserved, compare each final sentence with its
canonical/source claim. Carry its who/where/time/condition/approximation into
that same fact, including the population of a superlative and about/roughly
for a rounded ratio. Another fact, the caption or an internal subject is
not a substitute for a missing boundary. Do this after the last shortening,
not only on an earlier draft. Define an unfamiliar named product or object
on its line; a proper name plus a vague word such as icon is not a reveal.

For config1, default to the literal `Did you know?`, plain lines with blank lines, and one
natural CTA. Do not append a teaser or substitute a subject title merely
because the prompt names a topic. Honor an explicitly requested title/layout.
Correct myths directly without a repeated label parade. Follow explicit
title/layout/casing/emoji instructions. The 65–85-word preference is not a
minimum: do not pad a short card. Caption adds no unsupported claim; keep
hashtags and source evidence separate. Send `text_metrics: {}`; only server
metrics/rendering are authoritative.

## 5. Review and repair before save or revise

Read the complete hook–facts–CTA as a general reader. Check angle, varied
contributions, strong opening, middle, closing, naturalness and reference fit.
Check each final line against its source and dedup meaning, then check the
caption, config and assembled-copy budgets. Review the exact payload copy,
not a nicer version shown only in chat.

### Submission audit — including previews

Do a reader audition before counting: place the complete draft beside the selected
profile examples (two atlas cards for config1) and compare concrete reveals, changes of payoff and the
ending. Identify the weakest line. If it is a definition, routine processing,
vague ancient origin, ordinary adoption or design-upgrade connective tissue,
replace the selection rather than just its adjectives. A single lighter line
can breathe; several together make a lesson. Apply this within the existing
two directed repair rounds, not an extra unbounded process.

For history, distinguish a surprising use from an adoption milestone. A use
earns its place through an unexpected property, setting or consequence the
reader can picture, not merely the name of an institution that used it.
An unexplained historical nickname is not self-explanatory: state its meaning
in everyday words or select a clearer candidate. End with a fact worth retelling.
For food/body copy, scientific categories should reveal something in familiar
terms: "a different type of ..." can preserve a classification without "genus".
Keep brief useful terms such as "aroma compound". Do not turn ordinary anatomy,
processing or taxonomy into multiple filler lines.


Before returning copy or sending Write, check these on the final text:

- Every fact earns its place through a distinct reveal or concrete utility;
  no repeated care/protection beat, generic mechanism or ordinary-function filler.
- The opening earns attention immediately; the middle changes the reader
  payoff; the closing is a different memorable fact, not a generic summary.
- Comparison contains a worthwhile contrast, not just ordinary differences;
  its device can span adjacent lines or a common baseline. History does not
  reproduce the source's sequence even after dates are removed. Myth
  corrections sound spoken.
- Read each line aloud: concrete everyday words, no instructional chapter
  sequence, no unsupported causal explanation and no lost material qualifier.
- Count the actual hook, each fact, rendered facts block, CTA and assembled
  copy, including separators/prefixes. Check current config, not remembered
  defaults. A total under its limit does not excuse an overlong hook or CTA.
  If counting is unavailable, shorten conservatively and do not report
  invented exact metrics or declare all limits verified.

For `plain_lines` with N facts, the facts-block character count is the sum
of all fact lengths **plus 2 × (N − 1)** for blank-line separators.
Full copy adds hook length + CTA length + **4** more separator characters.
For example, seven lines totaling 493 characters make a 505-character facts
block, so they do not fit a 500-character block even when the full card fits.
Keep a small margin below hard limits when shortening; do not replace exact
counting with a claim that the average line looks short enough. This generic
layout example is not a fact, output metric assertion or new server rule.

These are internal editing checks, not extra user approval, payload fields
or server attestations. Local counts are a preflight; send `text_metrics: {}`
and treat server rendering as authoritative when a Write is authorized.

If it still reads like a textbook or monotonous specifications, repair before
`save_post_v2` or `revise_post_v2`, even if all three v2 checks could pass.
For each directed repair round, identify the defect, edit wording or research
and dedup replacements, then review the whole card again. After two directed
editorial repair rounds still fail, stop this attempt and report the limitation
and proven progress. Do not add filler, silently reduce count, force DISTINCT,
create a replacement post or begin a third automatic repair round.
Further work requires a newly resolved direction within the user's scope.
Do not invent another server attestation or error. No routine preview/GO checkpoint.

## 6. Refresh context, write once and read back

Only after the copy is ready, assess exactly `meaning_preserved`,
`caption_no_new_claim`, `no_known_substantive_issue` truthfully. All must pass;
the last means no known substantive issue, not a style or virality score.
Minor style preferences after the writing standard is met remain nonblocking.

Read current generation context immediately before fact-bearing Write.
Preserve config ID, six-digit timestamp and catalog string exactly, without
JavaScript Date conversion. If config changed, revalidate the final copy;
if catalog changed, refresh dedup and review. Re-read context before Write
after any further editing/research. Save within the scoped draft count or
revise the same authorized post with reviewed CAS revision/hash.

After success, `get_post` with sources/checks must match Page, facts/URLs,
rendered copy, status, revision/hash and checks. Compare the reviewed copy,
submitted copy and readback; a mismatch is not persisted DNA success.
Reconcile the same post under CAS and authorization, without a replacement.
Do not silently beautify readback for display. Unknown Write outcome requires
state reconciliation or identical operation key/semantic payload replay before
changed facts or a new operation. Respect claim expiry, one renewal, rate waits
and bounded retries in core. Approval remains explicit and separate.

## Walkthrough A — the diameter list

Input: a surprising comparison request, not an explicit diameter table.
Diagnosis: five independently supported diameters still give one repeated
experience. Read RP11 and RP12, then BA01. Retain the requested comparison
subject; research other source-supported, imaginable relationships, check
like-for-like scope within each pair, and dedup new candidates. Replace weak
measurement lines, order distinct payoffs and edit compact natural sentences.
Review the whole final card before context/Write. No invented analogy or number.
If the replacements cannot support the required count after two repair rounds,
report the shortfall; do not save the old list with a style note.

## Walkthrough B — two caregiving lines

Input: an animal cooperation card with two lines about different helpers
raising young. Diagnosis: literal claims may differ but repeat the same payoff.
Read RP03 and RP01, then BA04. Keep the stronger supported caregiving fact;
research a different observed communication, sensory or adaptation angle for
the same subject. Verify and dedup the replacement; do not infer human motives.
Reorder opening/middle/closing and review the exact copy before context/Write.
If the replacement is used or uncertain, find another; never force DISTINCT.
If two repair rounds still fail, report proven progress and remaining work
without saving filler or reducing the configured count.

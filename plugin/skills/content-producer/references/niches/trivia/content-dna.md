# Trivia content DNA — editorial decisions

## Authority and scope

Apply only to the enabled `trivia` niche. CR-061 updates the writer's obligations
under CR-053/054/059: finish DNA selection and editing **before** save/revise.
This is a plugin writing requirement, not another server check or approval gate.
Keep the three v2 attestations unchanged; `quality_status: passed` is not a DNA score.
Minor style preferences after the writing standard is met remain nonblocking.

Explicit user intent and current Page/item config control subject, count, format,
layout and hard budgets. Never change them to imitate an example. Five drafts
means five posts, not five facts per post. Respect a legitimate explicit fixed
count. When config permits, prefer 7–8 fast facts or 5–6 utility facts.
The 65–85-word target is a preference, not a minimum: never pad a short reveal.

Read [formats](formats.md), [fact checking](fact-checking.md),
[dedup ontology](dedup-ontology.md), and [quality rubric](quality-rubric.md).
The [core workflow](../../core/workflow.md) owns authorization, scope and lifecycle.
Frozen authority remains `plans/trivia-content-dna.md` and
`plans/module-contract.md`, with CR-061's limited editorial overrides.
The reference-patterns atlas provides complete examples; read its index and two
relevant cards plus a relevant anti-example before composing. Examples teach
decisions, not reusable production text.

## What the reader should experience

Write short, text-led Reels around a familiar thing with something unexpected
to discover. A 13-second loop need not contain an entire reading; it is not proof
of replay or retention. Give each line its own payoff: an unexpected ability,
a belief corrected, a vivid scale, an odd origin, or a useful overlooked detail.
Keep the tone warm, curious and credible for US, UK, Canada and Australia.

A topic is not an angle. Before research, state a brief editorial aim such as
"the strange things this everyday object can do" or "storage habits that change
texture." Do not default to summarizing an encyclopedia article.

## Breadth and angle walkthroughs

Use current Page weights and recent variety across a batch; these reference
proportions are guidance, not quotas for a small batch or an individual card:
animals/pets 30%, body/food 20%, practical utility 15%, places 15%,
nature/space 10%, history/inventions/people 10%.

| Familiar subject | Promising angle | Replace this weak direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body: saliva, sweat, digestion | A daily experience with a hidden mechanism | A list of organ functions or diagnoses |
| Food: eggs, coffee, potatoes | A cooking change, mistaken belief, unusual origin | A nutrition table with no reveal |
| Places: Japan, London, islands | Daily life, odd infrastructure, geography you can picture | Capitals, area and population in sequence |
| History: a familiar inventor | Unexpected activities, uses and choices | Birth–school–career chronology |
| Objects: fasteners, mirrors, packaging | An overlooked design or original purpose | Dates and patent numbers alone |
| Animals: dogs, octopuses | Senses, anatomy, behavior and human connection | Taxonomy, diet and habitat as filler |
| Nature: volcanoes, oceans | A concrete process or surprising consequence | Repeated dimensions or vague catastrophe |

For each row: retain the requested subject, research extra candidate angles,
verify them, then select a set with different payoffs. Body is not automatically
medical advice; utility is not automatically a safety lecture; animal emotion
must remain grounded in observed behavior. Do not escape a difficult requested
history/body/place topic by switching to animals or space.

Allowed `topic_key` values: `animals`, `pet_safety`, `food_science`,
`food_storage_home`, `practical_utility`, `human_body`, `nature_science`,
`geography`, `history_origins`, `inventions_everyday`, `general_trivia`.
A mixed collection needs an understandable umbrella and an honest active-format
mapping; do not invent a sixth format or disguise mixed trivia as one species.

## Select a collection, not merely valid sentences

Research more eligible candidates than needed. For each, identify its reveal
and distinct contribution in a short editorial note. A supported sentence can
still be too obvious to earn space. Replace filler rather than praising it.

- Vary the reader's experience as well as the literal proposition.
- Two different caregivers helping young may repeat the same payoff.
- Several vocal signals, or vigilance followed by its warning, can repeat
  one experience too. New behavior labels are not new reader payoffs.
- Two cities having different ordinary facilities is not a striking contrast.
  Select the unexpected feature first; do not manufacture novelty by pairing it.
- Five different diameters may still read as one repeated fact.
- A number earns space when it creates a meaningful relationship or image.
- Utility earns space through a concrete action, condition and outcome.
- A compact fact need not include an explanation or technical name.
- Do not impose one number, one emotional fact and one history fact on every card.
- Keep materially different claims distinct; never force DISTINCT to evade reuse.

Classify weak candidates as distinctive, useful context or obvious filler.
Replace elementary reminders that dilute the set, such as an animal breathing,
feeding or moving in its normal way. A surprising, useful condition can be
distinctive without dramatic language. One source may support several claims;
its paragraph order must not automatically become the card order.

## Order for attention

Open with a strong, independently understandable reveal. Vary the middle so each
line gives a reason to continue. Close on a different memorable fact.
Do not default to definition–anatomy–function, numerical ranking or source order.
Do not label an ordinary diet fact a "superpower." If two adjacent facts trigger
the same reaction, move or replace one. Preserve necessary procedural order.

Comparison must be like-for-like **within each pair**; dimensions may vary across
lines. A real contrast may also emerge across adjacent lines or an obvious
common baseline; two cities need not be forced into every line. Different
facilities alone do not make an interesting comparison. Vary sentence rhythm.
Correct myths directly; repeating `Myth:`/`Fact:` is not required unless
requested. A requested literal data table remains a table. Neither varied
comparison nor a stronger hook licenses new unsupported claims.

## Reveal-first American English

Use `en-US`, `conversational_credible`, concrete subjects and active everyday
verbs. Let the fact supply the surprise. Natural contractions are welcome.
Write one principal proposition per line with short explanation only when useful.

Lead with what a reader can picture, not a molecule, research method or academic
attribution. Retain technical names, dates and attributions when they are material.
Keep negation, quantity, species, location, conditions and uncertainty.
"Some trained dogs" cannot become "dogs"; "can" cannot become "always."
If honest context will not fit, choose another fact or angle.

Use numerals when they help. Choose units natural to the claim and audience;
dual units are optional, not mandatory on every line. Avoid regional slang,
stacked hedging, filler, fake shock, unsupported superlatives and assumed animal
motives. Follow Page casing/emoji settings. Do not replace source precision
with a catchy but false analogy.

Default hook: `Did you know?`. Plain fact lines separated by blank lines and
one short CTA. Honor an explicit title/layout. Choose save for practical value,
share for relevance to another person, comment for a real choice, follow for a
coherent series. Avoid stiff CTAs that ask people to share "group behaviors."
Caption adds no new unsupported factual claim; hashtags and internal sources
remain separate. See formats for component and assembled-copy budgets.

## Finish the writing before Write

Review angle, opening, middle contributions, closing, naturalness and reference
fit on the exact final copy. Replace weak facts for selection problems; edit
sentences for wording problems. Do not save a textbook card merely because the
server could accept it, or leave its core DNA problem as an after-save note.
Use the normal research/dedup/edit flow without a routine preview/GO checkpoint.

After two directed editorial repair rounds still fail, report the limitation
and proven progress. Do not add filler, reduce count silently, fake support,
force semantic distinctions or loop without bounds. The three v2 checks retain
their meanings; owner approval remains separate. Read back the same saved copy
before claiming persisted DNA fit.

## Pattern evidence is not fact evidence

The 176 reference records from Curious Mind, dyk and Daily Facts demonstrate
rhythm and selection patterns. Duplicates are not independent evidence; curated
million-view winners do not establish causal lift or a success rate.
Use account + POST-id for pattern provenance, never as factual source evidence.
Research every production claim independently under fact-checking policy.
Do not copy a whole competitor card, its sequence or source passages.
The atlas is not an eligible facts bank: production still needs fresh support
assessment, semantic dedup and current Page context.

System checks are deterministic server work; ChatGPT supplies truthful editorial
attestation; only the owner can explicitly approve the exact revision/hash.
Source tests, human review, previews and LIVE saved-copy evidence are different.

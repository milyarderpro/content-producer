# Trivia content DNA — editorial decisions

This policy applies only when the selected, enabled niche is `trivia`. Read [formats](formats.md), [fact checking](fact-checking.md), [dedup ontology](dedup-ontology.md), and [quality rubric](quality-rubric.md) as needed. The generic [core workflow](../../core/workflow.md) owns Page selection, authorization boundaries, reservations and post lifecycle. Frozen authority: `plans/trivia-content-dna.md` ``3–7, 10–14, 22–24; `plans/module-contract.md`.

## Editorial purpose

Build short static information Reels (approximately 13 seconds) around six qualities: a familiar anchor, meaningful (not exaggerated) surprise, one atomic claim per line, a coherent collection, visual scanability, and one natural interaction goal. A 13-second video is **not** a promise that viewers can read the entire card in one play. Prefer accurate, fresh, interesting facts over filling an exact quota.

Default language is neutral American English (`en-US`), understandable in the US, UK, Canada and Australia. Use American spelling, concrete active language, sentence case, familiar words, and `conversational_credible` tone. Avoid unexplained regional slang, claims of guaranteed virality, unnecessary emoji, ALL CAPS, fake shock, moral pressure and unsupported superlatives. Make numerals, units and comparisons precise; retain material populations, conditions, places, dates and uncertainty.

## Topic mix is guidance, not a quota

- Animals and pet safety 30%: senses, adaptation and observed behaviors, without unsupported animal motives.
- Human body and food science 20%: stable science and myth correction, never diagnosis or treatment.
- Practical utility and food storage/home 15%: low-risk context-supported explanations, never dangerous hacks.
- Geography and places 15%: explicit metric, region and date for changing comparisons.
- Nature, space and science 10%: scale and documented phenomena, no apocalyptic exaggeration.
- History, inventions, origins and people 10%: primary records and qualified disputed anecdotes.

Allowed `topic_key` values: `animals`, `pet_safety`, `food_science`, `food_storage_home`, `practical_utility`, `human_body`, `nature_science`, `geography`, `history_origins`, `inventions_everyday`, `general_trivia`. The last is for a structured mixed collection with a unifying device, not unrelated leftover claims or another niche. Avoid adjacent posts with the same subject/relation/emotional beat. User Page config, not this document, decides actual enabled settings.

## Choose and write

Score each *verified* candidate informally from 0–2 for familiar anchor, surprise, specificity, visualizability, utility/emotion, compactness and fit. This editorial score cannot override source, freshness, dedup or safety failures. Start with an independently understandable strong fact, vary relations and openings, and end with a memorable fact; do not add low-value filler. Aim for at least two high-surprise facts in a 5–6-fact post or three in a 7–9-fact post; at most one low-surprise context fact. These are editorial preferences, not deterministic server checks.

Default hook: `Did you know?` Alternative hooks must truthfully match the subject, count and format. A fact line conveys one principal proposition; compress by removing filler, not qualifiers. Choose a replacement fact when a critical qualifier cannot fit. Use a single CTA aligned to `share`, `save`, `comment` or `follow`. Facebook caption reinforces the theme without new unsourced claims; hashtags are relevant, non-spammy and separate from Canva copy. See [formats](formats.md) for exact length/layout limits.

## Pattern evidence is not fact evidence

The 176 Curious Mind, dyk and Daily Facts examples in the frozen reference dataset show rhythm, formats and failure patterns only. They are **not** factual citations, valid copying templates, proof of causal engagement, or a license to closely paraphrase. Research every new claim from independent eligible sources under [fact checking](fact-checking.md). Never paste a dataset/source passage into output. Report uncertain, unverified or duplicative claims as needing review or replacement.

System checks are deterministic server work; ChatGPT supplies semantic editorial judgment and attestation for the exact content hash; only the authenticated owner may expressly approve a draft. No policy paragraph or estimated score replaces these three authorities.
